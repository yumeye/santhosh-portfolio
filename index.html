Stage 01 / Mission

SAE Drone Development Challenge tasks teams with designing, building and flight-testing a fixed-wing UAV against a defined payload and packaging constraint. For the 2026 Micro Class aircraft, that meant a twin-motor airframe built to carry the highest payload fraction possible inside a fixed shipping box — the constraint that would go on to drive the redesign in Stage 04.

Stage 02 / Design

From configuration sketch to DFM-oriented CAD

The airframe is a twin-motor, high-wing configuration built around the S1223 airfoil, modelled in SolidWorks as DFM-oriented assemblies — ribs, mounts and hinges designed around how each part would actually be cut or printed, not fitted to manufacturing afterward.

Stage 03 / Analysis — Structural, SolidWorks Simulation

FEA before cutting a single rib

Static structural simulations in SolidWorks Simulation checked wing-spar displacement under representative flight loads before manufacturing began — root fixed, distributed lift and reaction loads applied outboard. Peak resultant displacement (URES) came out to roughly 0.26 mm at the wingtip on the spar model, and a full-airframe check under the same load logic showed peak displacement of roughly 0.83 mm concentrated at the wingtips and tail. Results directly informed spar sizing and rib spacing.

Stage 03 / Analysis — Aerodynamic, SolidWorks Flow Simulation

Checking calculated lift against simulation

Separately, external-flow CFD in SolidWorks Flow Simulation ran at 0° and 5° angle of attack, resolving a surface pressure field spanning roughly 100.7–102.8 kPa across the airframe, cross-checked against hand-calculated aerodynamic estimates before committing the design to manufacturing.

Stage 04 / Redesign

Analysis exposed a packaging constraint — I led the response

Midway through development, the competition's packaging box became the binding constraint on how much payload the aircraft could carry. I led the aircraft redesign, filed an Engineering Change Request (ECR), and drove a second design iteration aimed squarely at payload fraction — without giving up the structural margin Stage 03 had already validated.

Stage 05 / Build

From nested .dxf and .stl files to physical parts

Ribs and formation profiles were laser-cut from balsa and plywood sheet stock; structural brackets, mounts and hinges were FDM printed in PLA, PETG and TPU. Every batch was checked back against the CAD model before assembly to catch cutting or slicing errors early rather than during final build.

Stage 06 / Test

Validating simulation against the real structure

Before flight testing, the wing structure went through static beam load testing to check the physical build against the FEA predictions, and control surfaces were load-tested on their servos to confirm they'd hold under aerodynamic load — not just simulated load.
